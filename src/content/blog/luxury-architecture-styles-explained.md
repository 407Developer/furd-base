When clients come to FURD BASE with a vision for their luxury home or commercial development, two styles dominate the conversation: **Modern** and **Minimalist**. While these terms are often used interchangeably, they represent distinct architectural philosophies. Here's how we differentiate them — and how to choose the right one for your project.

## Modern Architecture

Modern architecture emerged from the early 20th-century movement that rejected historical ornamentation in favor of function-driven design. Key characteristics include:

- **Clean lines** with geometric precision
- **Large glass expanses** connecting interior and exterior
- **Open floor plans** with fluid spatial transitions
- **Exposed structural elements** celebrated as design features
- **Flat roofs** with cantilevered overhangs

At FURD BASE, our Modern projects emphasize the relationship between the built form and its environment. The Zenith Pavilion in Abuja CBD exemplifies this — a post-tensioned concrete structure with floor-to-ceiling Low-E double glazing and a floating staircase cantilever.

### Best For
- Clients who want their building to make a statement
- Sites with views worth framing (hillsides, waterfronts)
- Mixed-use commercial-residential developments

## Minimalist Architecture

Minimalism strips Modernism further, reducing architecture to its purest essence. The philosophy is "less is more" — every element must serve a purpose.

- **Monochromatic palettes** — whites, grays, warm earth tones
- **Absence of ornament** — no cornices, moldings, or decorative trim
- **Seamless surfaces** — flush doors, hidden hardware, concealed joinery
- **Deliberate emptiness** — space itself becomes the luxury
- **Material honesty** — concrete, glass, stone, and wood left in their natural finish

Our Obsidian Oasis project on Lekki Peninsula is a pure Minimalist work. A reinforced concrete frame with hot-dip galvanized steel accents, deep overhangs for coastal sun protection, and interior spaces defined by light and shadow rather than objects.

### Best For
- Clients seeking tranquility and visual calm
- Art collectors (architecture as gallery)
- Urban infill sites where the building must recede

## Which One Should You Choose?

| Factor | Modern | Minimalist |
|---|---|---|
| Statement | Bold, expressive | Quiet, refined |
| Material variety | High (glass, steel, concrete, stone) | Low (2-3 materials max) |
| Maintenance | Moderate (many surfaces) | Low (fewer details to maintain) |
| Cost per m² | Higher (complex geometries) | Moderate (simpler forms) |
| Best climate | Temperate to tropical | Any (with proper shading) |
